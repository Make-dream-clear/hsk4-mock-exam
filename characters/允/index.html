允 (yǔn) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

允
yǔn to grant, to consent; just, fair 4 strokes · Radical 儿 (son) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 允 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

允 (yǔn) means to grant, to consent; just, fair. It is written in 4 strokes, with the radical 儿 (son), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced yǔn.

  • to grant
  • to consent
  • just
  • fair

Decomposition

允 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Top + Bottom. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

允 =
厶 top private
儿 bottom son

Radical

The radical of 允 is 儿 — meaning son. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

Other HSK 4 characters that share this radical: 兄克光免.
