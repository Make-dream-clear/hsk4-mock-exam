醒 (xǐng) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

醒
xǐng to wake up, to startle; to sober up 16 strokes · Radical 酉 (wine) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 醒 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

醒 (xǐng) means to wake up, to startle; to sober up. It is written in 16 strokes, with the radical 酉 (wine), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced xǐng.

  • to wake up
  • to startle
  • to sober up

Decomposition

醒 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Left + Right. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

醒 =
酉 left wine

Radical

The radical of 醒 is 酉 — meaning wine. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

Other HSK 4 characters that share this radical: 酸.

How the character is built

醒 is a phono-semantic compound. The semantic component 酉 (wine) carries the meaning, while the phonetic component 星 originally indicated the sound.
