肯 (kěn) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

肯
kěn to agree to, to consent, to permit; ready, willing 8 strokes · Radical ⺼ (meat) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 肯 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

肯 (kěn) means to agree to, to consent, to permit; ready, willing. It is written in 8 strokes, with the radical ⺼ (meat), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced kěn.

  • to agree to
  • to consent
  • to permit
  • ready
  • willing

Decomposition

肯 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Top + Bottom. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

肯 = 止 top to stop
⺼ bottom meat

Radical

The radical of 肯 is ⺼ — meaning meat. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

Other HSK 4 characters that share this radical: 背膊肚肤膏胳脾脱.

How the character is built

肯 is an ideographic compound — its meaning is suggested by the combination of its parts rather than by sound. To stop 止 flexing one's muscles ⺼.
