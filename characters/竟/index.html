竟 (jìng) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

竟
jìng finally, after all, at last; indeed; unexpected 11 strokes · Radical 立 (to stand) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 竟 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

竟 (jìng) means finally, after all, at last; indeed; unexpected. It is written in 11 strokes, with the radical 立 (to stand), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced jìng.

  • finally
  • after all
  • at last
  • indeed
  • unexpected

Decomposition

竟 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Top + Bottom. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

竟 =

Radical

The radical of 竟 is 立 — meaning to stand. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

Other HSK 4 characters that share this radical: 章竞亲童.

How the character is built

竟 is an ideographic compound — its meaning is suggested by the combination of its parts rather than by sound. A person 儿 finishing a musical piece 音.
