切 (qiè) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

切
qiè to cut, to mince, to slice, to carve; close to; eager 4 strokes · Radical 刀 (knife) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 切 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

切 (qiè) means to cut, to mince, to slice, to carve; close to; eager. It is written in 4 strokes, with the radical 刀 (knife), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced qiè.

  • to cut
  • to mince
  • to slice
  • to carve
  • close to
  • eager

Decomposition

切 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Left + Right. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

切 =
七 left seven
刀 right knife

Radical

The radical of 切 is 刀 — meaning knife. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

Other HSK 4 characters that share this radical: 刀.

How the character is built

切 is a phono-semantic compound. The semantic component 刀 (knife) carries the meaning, while the phonetic component 七 originally indicated the sound.
