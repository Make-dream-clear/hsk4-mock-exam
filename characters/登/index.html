登 (dēng) — HSK 4 Recognition Character: Meaning, Radical & Stroke Order

登
dēng to rise, to mount, to board, to climb 12 strokes · Radical 癶 (legs) · HSK 4 recognition character (认读字)
👁 Recognition only: the official HSK 4 syllabus asks you to recognize 登 when reading — handwriting it is not required (that applies to the 150 writing characters). The stroke animation below is optional but helps memory.
Quick Answer

登 (dēng) means to rise, to mount, to board, to climb. It is written in 12 strokes, with the radical 癶 (legs), and is one of the 441 HSK 4 recognition characters (认读字) — you must recognize it when reading, though handwriting it is not required.

Pronunciation & meaning

Pronounced dēng.

  • to rise
  • to mount
  • to board
  • to climb

Decomposition

登 breaks down into 2 components arranged as Top + Bottom. Recognizing the components makes the character easier to remember and write.

登 =
癶 top legs
豆 bottom beans

Radical

The radical of 登 is 癶 — meaning legs. Radicals are the indexing components used in Chinese dictionaries; they often hint at a character's broad meaning category.

How the character is built

登 is a phono-semantic compound. The semantic component 癶 (legs) carries the meaning, while the phonetic component 豆 originally indicated the sound.
